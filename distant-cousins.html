    Where it all comes from

    The tree is Wikidata's: every animal it knows and who its parents are, which thousands of people keep up to date. The dates come from the fossils in the Paleobiology Database: a fossil's age is the age of the rock it's in, which is often known only to within a few million years, so the page says "at least" where it can't be sure. Weights for mammals and birds are the averages scientists collected in EltonTraits, and for everything else Wikidata's grown-up figures. The words come from Wikipedia, the pictures from Wikimedia Commons (credited under each one) and the silhouettes from PhyloPic (credited on the card, where the artist asks for it). Scientists have named thousands of in-between groups, so only the well known ones are kept here: the rest are folded into the branch above. Scientists also still argue about plenty of branches, so now and then a creature sits somewhere surprising.

    Everyone's favourites, the daily cousin and how your tree compares with everybody else's go through a little server of mine on Cloudflare. Nobody signs in: your browser makes up a random number to tell your visits apart, and nothing that could say who you are is kept.
